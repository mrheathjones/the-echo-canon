# THE ECHO — External Review Prompt (Go / No-Go: is this worth committing to?)

## Why you're being asked
An author is deciding whether to commit a serious chunk of their life to writing this series. They do not want encouragement — they want the truth, because a false "yes" costs them years and a false "no" kills something real. Your job is to give a clear, calibrated, defensible read on one question: **Is there a genuine, commit-worthy series here — and does the work so far prove it?** Treat this as a green-light decision, not a polish pass.

## What you're looking at (so you judge the right thing)
This is **not a finished manuscript.** It is a proof-of-concept package: a locked story architecture (canon, magic system, characters, Book 1 spine, series endgame) plus **eight load-bearing scenes drafted to demonstrate the hardest parts of the book.** The scenes span the arc rather than running continuously; roughly the first 25% and most connective tissue are deliberately unwritten. So judge **viability and proof-of-concept, not completeness.** The question is not "is this done" — it is "is this worth finishing, and has the author shown they can finish it well."

Materials (repo, branch `draft`): read the review brief (`review/THE_ECHO_REVIEW_BRIEF.md`) first for premise and architecture; then the eight scenes under `drafts/scenes/`; then dip into `canon/` to stress-test the architecture.

## Be the right kind of reviewer
- You are independent. Assume the people who built this are too close to it to see its real flaws; your value is outside eyes.
- The kindest thing you can do is be accurate. Do not inflate to be nice, and do not trash to seem rigorous.
- Separate two judgments that fail differently: **(a) is the concept/architecture viable** (is this book worth writing) and **(b) is the execution proven** (can this author write *this* book). A commit needs both. If one is strong and the other isn't, say so plainly — that is the most common and most useful finding.
- Avoid filler. A generic "ten strengths / ten weaknesses" list dodges the decision. Answer the decision.

## The questions that actually determine the verdict
1. **Is the heart proven?** The series rests on the Lucas–Nolan brother relationship. Do the drafted brother scenes (store run → recoil → the friends' correction → the acceptance) make you *feel* it — does the rupture land, is the acceptance earned, would a real reader ache? If this is not proven, nothing else matters.
2. **Is the architecture sound and sustainable across multiple books?** Stress-test the engine: magic-as-perception-and-flaw; the Gray-as-mercy threat (a non-evil institution); and especially the endgame — the "schism," and the "second clock" in which a *healing* founder becomes more dangerous, not less. Does it hold, or collapse by Book 3? Is it *generative* (keeps producing story) or a clever closed box that wears out?
3. **Does it actually differentiate?** Be honest about the upper-middle-grade market. Does the combination — intact loving family, perception-as-burden, a neurodivergent (ADHD + dyslexia) lead, threat routed through a beloved sibling — genuinely stand out, or is it competent-but-crowded? Give real comps and say where this sits against them.
4. **Voice and theme.** Is the close-third-at-13 voice real and sustainable over a whole book? Does it deliver its theme *without preaching* (its own stated cardinal rule)? Is the neurodivergent representation lived or decorative?
5. **The failure mode.** Name the single thing most likely to sink this — and say plainly whether it is **fixable or fatal.**

## Deliver, in this order
1. **The verdict, first, in one paragraph.** An explicit recommendation on this scale — **Commit / Commit with specific reservations / Rework the premise before committing / Walk away** — and the single biggest reason for it. Lead with this; do not bury it.
2. **Is the heart proven?** Your honest reader-gut on the brother spine, not just a craft assessment.
3. **Does the architecture hold and sustain a series?** Your endgame / second-clock stress-test — where it is strongest and where it is most likely to break.
4. **Does it differentiate?** Market-honest, with comps.
5. **The biggest risk** — fixable or fatal; if fixable, the one move that most de-risks it.
6. **Realistic ceiling and floor.** If executed competently from here, what is the best-case outcome and the most-likely one? Be concrete.
7. **If you would walk, say why, plainly. If you would commit, name the one or two things to protect at all costs** — the elements that are the actual reason this is worth doing.

Do not focus on line-level prose polish, and do not penalize the absence of unwritten sections. Focus on whether there is a real, distinctive, sustainable series here, and whether the work so far proves the author can deliver it. The author can take a no. Give them the true answer.
