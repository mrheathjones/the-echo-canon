## Positioning

- **Format:** illustrated prose hybrid (prose for family/interiority/adult tragedy; full-bleed art for the visual metaphors — the Gray, Echo Weather, manifestations).
- **Category/age:** upper middle grade; protagonist Lucas is 13.
- **Logline:** A 13-year-old who can see the hidden emotional layer of the world is the only one who notices when his magnetic older brother starts quietly vanishing into a kind, well-meaning institution that calls erasure "healing" — and his certainty that he alone is right nearly costs him the brother he's trying to save.
- **Comps:** *Amari and the Night Brothers*, *Nevermoor*; *A Wrinkle in Time* (Camazotz) as the thematic touchstone for the Gray.
- **Rep:** ADHD **and** dyslexia, both load-bearing and lived; the hero is not broken, and is often *wrong*.

## The world, briefly

The Echo is a hidden emotional layer of reality, objectively real but perceived differently by each mind. Lucas filters less, so he catches more — a burden, not a superpower. **Attunement** is growth available to everyone, dramatized as choices, never spellcasting.

**The magic is perception, not power.** Scenes are won by seeing correctly and acting on it; lost by misreading and acting on the misread — which is also Lucas's core flaw ("being misunderstood doesn't mean you're right"). The verbs: **Catch → Read → Hold → Name → Choose.** Resolution (**Option B**): naming a manifestation *reveals* what it needs; a *costly choice* — usually by the person it belongs to — *resolves* it. Wins look like preserved aliveness, never calm.

**The Gray** is the institution's flattening, experienced by the grayed as peace. It is **intended-as-mercy**: the Accord sincerely believes quieting is healing. Mechanically, the Gray is the relief taken when someone is named truly and *declines* the costly choice.

**The Accord** is a legitimate, non-cartoonish institution founded from grief (the Nathan / Adrian / Sarah tragedy — kept off-page in Book 1). Its danger is method and endpoint, not malice.

## Series engine + endgame (NOW LOCKED — was the open v2 question)

Previously "unresolved — chew on it." Now canon (`canon/00_SERIES_DECISIONS_LOCKED.md` decisions 8–9; `canon/world/accord/POWER_STRUCTURE.md`; the founders' dossiers):

- **The second clock (the engine after the founding grief resolves):** control of the Gray is split — **Adrian (the Architect) owns its design/philosophy; Nathan (the Curator) owns its scale/reach.** Nathan heals in **two stages**: first, *self-forgiveness* makes him evangelical ("bring it to everyone") → foot off the brake → the Gray spreads *faster* → "does the lost brother come back?" becomes a **moving deadline traveling toward him.** **The healed founder is *more* dangerous.** Only *later* does his healing mature into rediscovering the value of mess — and that's when he turns against the design.
- **The endgame = SCHISM** (not reform, not a faction-war-to-win): the magic system at institutional scale. The Accord splits between those who can **hold not-knowing** and those who can't (the friend-group's own verb, scaled up); both wings sympathetic; **no villain to defeat**; the choice propagates person-by-person the way the Gray does.

**Pressure-test this — don't re-propose it.** Is "the healed founder is more dangerous" → schism a sound, *dramatizable* multi-book engine, or clever-on-paper? Where is it weakest?

## Cast (Book 1)

The Hale family — loud, intact, loving, imperfect (a deliberate differentiator from orphan-MG). **Nolan, 19** (recent HS grad, adrift), the beloved, manipulative-but-lovable older brother, is Book 1's emotional engine and the A-plot's heart. (Siblings: Jack 21, Emma 19 — Nolan's twin, Lucas 13, Dylan ~10.) Core friends **Maya Sinclair, Zoe Bennett, Mason Reed, Eli Sullivan** (surnames locked-proposed), each owning a distinct role in solving Echo problems (reader-of-people / driver / anchor / reader-of-systems) so the *group*, not Lucas alone, does the work. **Abby Whitmore**, the founder's daughter, the established insider. **Devon Cross**, a sincere young-adult-track **Guide** (the Accord's warm word for a recruiter) and the Book-1 antagonistic force — himself an Accord "success story," a *counterfeit* of the clean older brother Lucas never got.

## Book 1 spine (full beat sheet: `canon/story/BOOK_1_SPINE.md`)

Two plots that are one. **A-plot:** Nolan grays; Lucas alone sees it; his certainty drives a rash "rescue" that makes it worse; he learns he cannot *choose for* Nolan — only stop treating him like the problem and leave the door open. **B-plot:** Abby's suppressed, unfinished self manifests and is resolved not by defeating it but by Abby's costly choice ("I don't know what I want to be"). One line: **Lucas practices the truth on a near-stranger and graduates by failing to force it on his brother.**

## The drafted skeleton (8 scenes, in `drafts/scenes/`)

The load-bearing beats — read them as a body; they're meant to prove the spine works.

- `act1_brother_store_run.md` — Act-1 warmth bank + lived dyslexia; the store-run intimacy ("new system / don't tell Mom") that later beats invert.
- `act2a_abby_seed.md` ("The Settling") — what Abby's settled self *buys* her (Nathan's approval, Adrian's pre-carved "slot," status, belonging); seeds the cost of her Act-3 choice.
- `act2a_devon_persuasive_scene.md` — the seduction; Devon sees Lucas truly; the reader should half-agree (his dead-calm "tell" is withheld here on purpose).
- `act2a_midpoint.md` — the hinge: Lucas connects dead-calm / campus Gray / Nolan's "calm" as one thing; it lands as **shame** that curdles into the certainty arming his mistake.
- `act2b_recoil.md` — the backfire: Lucas names Nolan a *victim*; Nolan — **choosing, not puppeted** — weaponizes the banked closeness, punches down once, and flinches.
- `act2b_maya_eli_correction.md` — the friends correct Lucas on evidence his gift **can't reach** (Nolan covered his own tracks to keep his access); *victim → chooser*. The fix for secret-superpower drift — Lucas is flatly *wrong about what's true*.
- `act3_garden_climax.md` — the distributed engine as **living mess** (roles cross lanes and misfire); Option B in action; Abby chooses; win ≠ calm.
- `act3_nolan_climax.md` — the A-plot peak: the **opposite of a rescue** — Lucas owns his part, names without forcing, accepts he can't choose for Nolan. Closes on "didn't go after it. For once."

## What we want pressure-tested (v3 — current state)

1. **The series engine (now locked — stress it, don't propose it).** Is the two-stage "healed founder is *more* dangerous" → schism engine sound and *dramatizable* across books, or clever-on-paper? 2. **Does the skeleton prove the spine?** Read the 8 scenes as one body: does the **Lucas–Nolan relationship** — the thing every prior review said determines whether the book works — actually land across the store-run → recoil → correction → Nolan-climax line?
3. **Preaching.** The cardinal rule is "don't preach." The per-character lesson matrix was just moved *out* of canon to reduce the risk. Do the scenes still show-not-tell, or does a lesson-delivery scent escape anyway?
4. **The ensemble / Nolan / Devon.** Do the four friends read as a living ensemble or a mechanical party of roles? Is Nolan lovable-not-harmless (cost under the charm; choosing, not puppeted)? Is Devon persuasive enough that you half-agree?
5. **Altitude + rep.** Right for upper MG with a 13-year-old lead, neither older nor younger? Is ADHD/dyslexia lived (not labeled), and is "hero is often wrong" handled without shaming?
6. **The biggest undrafted risk.** Act 1 (opening image, Hale-dinner ordinary world, the inciting "double door," debate, break-into-two) is essentially unwritten and is the reader's front door. Given the drafted skeleton, **what must Act 1 establish that the later scenes are leaning on but haven't earned yet?**
7. **Dramatization debts.** Canon already flags one (Nathan needs his own on-page "garden" — a moment where mess *saves* something — or his Phase-2 turn reads as thesis). Are there others the skeleton is quietly deferring?
