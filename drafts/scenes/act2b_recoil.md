# Book 1 — Act 2B: The Recoil (Lucas & Nolan) — proof-of-concept draft

POV: close third, anchored on Lucas, narrator held at 13 (he feels, doesn't diagnose).
Position: Act 2B — the backfire of the rash rescue. Lucas comes home from the midpoint armed
with certainty and tries to drag Nolan out: he names Nolan a *victim*, names it as something
being *done* to him. Nolan — who is choosing this — recoils, weaponizes the banked store-run
closeness (solidarity reframed as pity), and lands the one barb that punches *down* (the
dyslexia) before half-registering the cost. The rescue pushes Nolan further toward the place
that doesn't treat him as the problem. Mess, not system: the Echo machine stays off the page,
fought in human terms. ≤1 thesis line; Lucas's anger stays grief, never contempt. Bookended on
the lived dyslexia — words sliding off, at the start and the end.

---

He'd had the whole thing ready on the bus — the true thing he was going to say, the words in the right order — and in his head it worked: he said it and Nolan heard it and whatever had hold of his brother let go. He had it the whole way up the driveway. Then he was standing in the open garage, and Nolan was crouched over his board with the little Allen key, tightening a truck, and the words slid off the way words on a page slid off, gone before Lucas could land on one.

"You're back," Nolan said, not looking up. "How was scout camp." He'd been calling the cohort scout camp all week. It stuck, the way everything Nolan named stuck.

"Can you stop." It came out too high. "I need to talk to you."

"So talk. I'm not going anywhere." Which wasn't true. The keys were already on the workbench, and the board meant out, and out meant the same place it had meant for weeks.

"That place you've been going." Lucas made himself say it. "Devon. All of it. There's something — they're doing something to you. I've been watching it. You're not even—" the word he wanted slipped and the wrong one came up "—you're not all the way *here* anymore. It's like somebody's got a hand on you and they're turning you down, a little at a time, and you can't see it because you're inside it, but I can, and I'm not gonna just stand here and let them—"

Nolan set the Allen key down.

He did it slow — the way he'd gone quiet in the checkout line that day, the way he went still right before he stopped being funny. He stood up. He wasn't smiling, exactly, but the shape of it was there, the one he used on teachers.

"Turning me down," he said. "Huh." He wiped his hands on his jeans. "And you came all the way home to save me. From what — from feeling okay for once?"

"That's not—"

"No, I wanna hear the plan. You drag me out of there, and then what. I come back here and we're all one big happy—" He stopped. Something cold came in under it. "You know what it's like over there? Nobody's on me. Nobody's standing around waiting for me to wreck it so they can do the face. The patient one. You know it." He did. "First place in my whole life nobody's got a list of everything wrong with me." A beat, soft, aimed. "And here you come. With a list."

It hit somewhere under Lucas's ribs and he couldn't even say why — except *list* was their word. *List* was the store. And Nolan knew it.

"That's not you talking," Lucas said. "That's them. They took it — the part of you that—"

"They took it." Nolan laughed, short, not real. "Right. Because there's no chance Nolan just wanted out. Somebody had to *do* it to me. Gotta be a them." He took a step. "Nobody did anything to me, Lucas. I picked it. Say it back so you hear it — I *picked* it. For once I got something that's mine, and you can't stand that it's not yours to fix."

"You're a victim," Lucas said. Too fast. Too loud. The word out before he could stop it. "You're a victim and you can't even *see* it—"

And the garage went quiet.

Nolan looked at him. Not angry. That was the thing — not angry, just done, the way you look at a door right before you shut it.

"A victim." Flat. Then, easy, like he was only clearing something up: "You wanna know what that actually was? The two of us, the store, *don't tell Mom*?" He spread his hands. "That was me feeling sorry for my little brother who can't read a sign in a grocery store. That's all it was. I covered for you 'cause somebody had to, and you went and turned it into some *us*." He shook his head. "There's no us. There's a kid I drove around."

It went out of Lucas all at once, like a foot coming down on a step that wasn't there. They never said it out loud. That was the entire point — the thing that made it work was that you *never* said it. And Nolan was saying it — out loud, on purpose, taking it apart in his hands so Lucas had to watch.

"And now—" Nolan's voice finally picked up, one spike of it "—the kid who can't get through a cereal box is gonna stand in my garage and read *me*."

The heat came up Lucas's neck — the exact heat from that aisle under the big lights, the collar-heat. Except in the store Nolan had walked straight into it and made it vanish, and now Nolan was the one holding the match.

And Nolan saw what he'd done. Lucas watched it cross him — the flicker, there and gone, the same look as when his phone buzzed in the checkout and he put it away without answering. For half a second his brother was in there. Then it closed over.

"Go inside," Nolan said. Quiet again. Quiet was worse. "You're just like the rest of them."

He picked up the keys. He didn't slam anything — Nolan never had to. He walked past close enough that Lucas felt the air move, and got in the car, and Lucas couldn't make a single word come.

Then he backed down the driveway and took the turn at the bottom slow. Careful. Both hands. About four miles an hour — the way Gramps did it, the way Nolan never did. He used to take that turn hard on purpose, just to hear Lucas laugh. He wasn't trying to make anybody laugh now.

Lucas stood in the open garage and watched his brother drive off like a careful stranger, and that was when the words finally came — all of them, in the right order, every true thing he'd had ready on the bus — and not one of them any use, sliding off one after another before he could catch a single one.
