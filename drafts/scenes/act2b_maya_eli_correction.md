# Book 1 — Act 2B: The Correction (Maya & Eli) — proof-of-concept draft

POV: close third, anchored on Lucas, narrator held at 13 (he feels, doesn't diagnose).
Position: Act 2B, the evening of the recoil — Lucas bikes out to Eli's Barn instead of going inside.
Both friends; Maya-anchored. Lucas comes in to build a rescue; the rescue dies in his hands.
(Setting = Eli's Barn, the only barn — on the Sullivans' property, not the Hales' suburban home.) Eli states the undeniable fact
(systems-legible — a thing Lucas's gift can never see); Maya lands the part that hurts. This is
the W4 discharge: Lucas is flatly *wrong about what's true* (Nolan is not a victim) and corrected
on the merits — so the friends become perceptually necessary and the gifted-seer drift is fixed.
The proof inverts the store-run "I covered for you / don't tell Mom": now Nolan covers for himself,
against them. Victim → chooser, which makes it scarier, not safer. NOT the climax — ends on
disorientation, not catharsis. ≤1 thesis line; no Echo verbs/weather; anger stays grief.

---

He didn't go inside. The house meant Mom, and Mom meant a face he didn't have left — so he got on his bike and rode out to Eli's instead, standing on the pedals the whole way, rebuilding the case as he went. Nolan wasn't himself. Somebody had a hand on him. You could see it if you knew how to look — and Lucas knew how to look, that was the part nobody got, that it wasn't him being dramatic, it was him being the only one in the room with the lights on. He had it all lined up by the time he dropped the bike in the grass outside the Barn. He just needed to say it to people who'd believe him.

Maya was on the busted couch with her knees up. Eli was at the bench with his laptop open between two dead drones, three tabs of something gray and boring on the screen. Neither of them said hey. That was the first wrong note — they'd been talking before he came in, and they'd stopped, and now they were both looking at him like he was the thing they'd been talking about.

"Okay, so I went," Lucas said. "To Nolan. And he's worse, way worse, it's like there's somebody else driving and he's just sitting in the back. We have to get him out before — I don't even know, before there's nothing left to get out. I've got most of it figured. I just need you guys in it before it slides off the way everything—"

"He signed himself up," Eli said. He didn't look up.

It went past Lucas like a car he didn't see coming. "What?"

"The young-adult track. There's an enrollment thing. I've been poking at it like two weeks — worse security than my grandma's pill app, it's embarrassing." Eli said it flat, no padding, the way he read part numbers off a box, the way it never occurred to him a thing might need softening. "When you sign up under twenty-one they can't just take you. The house has to okay it. A code gets texted to the family number — the one that rings every phone on the plan — and somebody reads it back. That's the yes." He tapped the screen. "Your mom never said it. Well — she got the text. She just never saw it."

"So they skipped her. So they're cheating, that's literally my whole—"

"They didn't skip it." Eli's voice didn't move, and the not-moving was the part that started to scare Lucas. "Code went out March nineteenth, 6:52. Answered 6:53, from Nolan's phone. Then that one message got pulled off the thread at 6:55. Just that one." He shrugged. "The log doesn't guess."

The fan pushed oil-smell across the room. Somewhere the string lights buzzed at the pitch Lucas felt in his back teeth.

"That's — okay, that's a glitch, the app did it, those apps do stuff—"

"There's more." Eli clicked. "Same account, somebody switched the phone bill from paper to email-only — so it stops coming in the mail, where your mom opens it. That's not a thing that happens by itself. It's like six taps, you have to go find it." Another click. "And the emergency contact. It was your mom — Rachel Hale, her cell, I know it off your fridge. Day after you guys fought in the garage, that got changed too. Mom off. Nolan on. He's his own emergency contact now." He sat back. "Same login, three times, three months. That field's buried — nobody finds it by accident."

"That doesn't—" Lucas started, and his mouth got ahead of his stomach, and his stomach already knew. *The house number. The one that rings every phone.* He knew exactly how that worked. He'd helped set it up. He could see his own thumb in it. *New system. You text me before we leave the house.* Nolan was the one who knew which messages Mom checked and which ones she never, ever looked at twice. Nolan was the one who'd say *she doesn't need our whole business* and mean it like a kindness.

He'd answered for himself. He'd answered for himself, and then he'd gone in and wiped it clean, the way you wipe a counter so nobody knows you were even in the kitchen.

"That's still—" His voice came out wrong, too fast. "That's exactly what it'd look like, though, if they got to him. That's the whole move, that's how it *works*, they make you cut your mom off, they isolate you — I read about it — this is them, this is them doing it through him—"

"Lucas." Maya. Just his name, low, and it landed harder than Eli's whole list, because Maya never aimed.

"No, because you didn't see him, somebody's *doing* this, he wouldn't take Mom off, he covered for *me*, the store thing, *don't tell Mom*, that was — that was *us*—"

And that was where it cracked, because he heard it land and heard what it meant in the same half-second, with no room to look away.

*Don't tell Mom. She doesn't need our whole business.* The two of them, the cart, the list, on the inside of the same small scam. Nolan handling the words so Lucas never had to stand red-faced under the big lights. Covering *for* him. And here was the exact same move — the quiet, the careful, *keep it from Mom, new system* — except Nolan had turned it all the way around, so the inside was just him, and Lucas was out on the step with everybody else.

The same hands. The same *don't-tell-Mom*. Pointed the other way now. Pointed at them.

"He could've—" Lucas heard how small it came out. "Somebody else could've—"

"It was his number. It was his thumbs." Eli shrugged, not unkind, just done. "He let himself in and locked it behind him."

Maya had come off the couch. She stood close without touching him, and her face was doing the thing it did right before she committed to a read and hated it, because committing meant she might be wrong, and being wrong meant letting somebody down. He watched her decide to do it anyway. For him. Which somehow made it worse.

"Think about who Nolan covers for," she said. Quiet. The room got quieter to let her. "Ever. In his whole life." She let it sit. "It's not Devon. He didn't do six taps so *Devon* wouldn't find out." She breathed. "And it's not the Accord hiding it from your mom — Lucas, they *want* parents, it's better for them when she knows. The only person who needed her not to find out was the one who didn't want to get pulled back."

"You knew," Lucas said. It wasn't really a question.

She didn't dodge it. "I thought it might be. A while ago. I didn't say." Her voice caught, and she pushed through it, gentle as a hand on a bruise. "If I was wrong, you'd never forgive me. If I was right—" She looked at him instead of away, which cost her. "I didn't want to be right."

And there it was, the whole rescue going out of Lucas like air. You couldn't drag somebody through a door he'd locked from the inside. *I picked it* — Nolan had said it in the garage, flat, twice, and Lucas had stood there and called it the place talking through him. It wasn't the place. It was Nolan.

His eyes were doing something hot. He turned, and that put him facing Maya, which was worse, because Maya didn't miss anything.

"He's not happening *to*, Lucas," she said. "I know that's the one you wanted. The one where it's not really him." She breathed. "He's choosing it. With both hands." And then, softer — like she'd decided to hand him the rest, and then decided not to — "And the part you can't stand—" She stopped. She let it go unfinished, which was its own kind of mercy, and somehow worse than finishing it.

It went in under everything anyway.

He opened his mouth to argue, to lay it out in the right order so she'd see — and nothing came up but the heat behind his eyes, and the lights, and the fan, all of it too much at once. And under it, small and stupid, the thing he kept seeing wasn't a stranger's hand, or a hold on his brother, or any of it. It was just Nolan's thumb. The actual one. The one that plucked the list out of his hand in the cereal aisle and made the whole hot mess of it disappear. Same thumb. Deleting the text. Like it was nothing. Like it was easy.

Eli, who didn't always catch when a thing had gone quiet for a reason, said, "I can show you the actual log. If you want. It's pretty clear," and Maya said, "*Eli*," low, and Eli said, "what," and then, a beat late, closed the laptop so the gray tabs went away.

Somewhere outside, Lil was probably listening, and for once Lucas hoped she was, because he needed one person in the world to say this was insane and be right.

"I gotta—" he said, and didn't finish, and went for the door. Maya didn't stop him. She got there first and held it open, and the cool outside air came in and met him halfway, and nobody said the thing that would make it okay, because there wasn't one.

Behind his eyes the one thing that wouldn't slide off kept playing anyway: Nolan at the bottom of their driveway, taking the turn slow. Careful as a stranger. Both hands.
