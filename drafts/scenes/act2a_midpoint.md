# Book 1 — Act 2A Midpoint (proof-of-concept draft)

POV: close third, anchored on Lucas.
Position: end of Act 2A — the false-victory hinge. Lucas accepts Devon's "just us" invitation, gets the seen-ness he's starved for, and the realization detonates mid-bliss: Devon's dead-calm = the campus Gray = Nolan's "rest." It lands as shame, not deduction, and curdles into the certainty that arms his Act 2B mistake.

---

He went. That was the part he'd never be able to take back.

He'd told himself a hundred reasons on the way over — reconnaissance, keeping an eye on Nolan's world, you can't know a thing you won't look at — and every one of them was true, and not one of them was the real one. The real one was smaller and worse. He wanted to. He wanted the just-us. He'd wanted it since the kitchen.

And Devon delivered. No clipboards, like he'd promised, no tour-guide voice. He met Lucas at a side door and they just walked, and Devon asked him real questions and waited for the real answers, and when Lucas said something fast and sideways the way his brain made him, Devon didn't blink or slow him down or do the patient face adults did. He just kept up. Nobody kept up. Nolan used to keep up, back when —

Lucas shoved that down and let himself have the afternoon.

The place was quiet in a way that felt incredible after a week of catching everything. His whole life was volume — the house, the cohort, the Echo throwing colors at him off every wall. Here the volume was just off. He could hear himself think. He hadn't heard himself think in so long he'd forgotten it was a sound. He felt his shoulders come down from around his ears, and a part of him he was ashamed of even while it spoke said, clear as a bell: *I could stay here. I could feel like this all the time.*

They came out into a courtyard. Garden, sort of — stone and gravel and Mr. Mercer's little carved animals on every ledge, the owls and foxes and the turtle that were all over campus. Devon stopped to let him take it in, easy, hands in his pockets, at home.

And Lucas, loose for the first time in weeks, made the mistake of actually looking.

Not wanting-looking. Catching-looking — the thing his brain did for free, that he'd been holding shut all afternoon because he didn't want it to wreck this. He let it open, just to see the pretty quiet garden properly.

The quiet wasn't quiet. The quiet was gray.

Not a color, exactly. An absence, where the Echo should have had weather and didn't. The courtyard was a dead spot, a hole in the world cut to the exact shape of peace, and the longer he held his eyes open the more he saw it wasn't empty — it was full of the heavy kind of stillness, the kind that pressed. The carvings sat in it, finished, every one of them, sanded smooth and done. A phrase drifted up off some campus poster he'd walked past a dozen times: *become what you were meant to be.* The little animals had. They'd become it. And then they'd stopped.

He turned to say something to Devon, he didn't even know what — and saw it on him too.

The clean still circle. The no-weather. He'd clocked it in the kitchen and called it *centered.* Called it *calm.* Called it the thing he wanted, because he wanted it. But it wasn't calm. It was the courtyard. It was the same gray, walking around inside a person, perfectly at home in a dead spot because it *was* one. Devon didn't have peace. Devon had been finished. Somebody had sanded him smooth and called it done, and Devon was so grateful he'd come back to do it to other people.

And Lucas's stomach dropped out of him, because he knew that gray. He'd been seeing it for weeks. He'd been watching it across the dinner table, thinning the corner where his brother stood, draining Nolan a shade at a time while the whole family clapped, because Nolan was finally calm, Nolan was finally fine, Nolan was finally —

*Finishing.*

It was all the same thing. The campus and the recruiter and his brother and the lovely quiet he'd been sitting in all afternoon with his shoulders down, thinking *I could stay here.* The relief. The blessed off-switch. He had been five minutes from wanting the exact thing that was eating Nolan alive. He'd *come* here because he wanted it.

He felt sick. Not scared-sick. Ashamed-sick — the kind that needs somewhere to go, fast, or it turns and goes for you.

"Hey." Devon was reading his face, kind as ever, no idea — or every idea. "You okay? You went somewhere."

He could have told him. Could have said the true thing, the held thing, the thing Maya would have made him sit inside until it was real. Instead the shame found the door and came out wearing the version he could live with: not *I almost wanted this,* but *they're doing it on purpose, and I'm the only one who can see it, and I have to stop it.*

Righteousness was so much easier to carry than shame. It stood him straight up. It pointed him at the exit. By the time he heard his own voice saying "I'm fine, I gotta go, my mom's—," he was already moving, already half-running, Devon's puzzled, gentle voice falling away behind him, and the shame was gone — scrubbed clean, swapped out for something hard and bright and absolutely certain of itself. He was the only one who saw it. So it was his. All of it. He'd get Nolan out if he had to drag him by the collar, he'd show every last one of them what the Accord really was, he'd prove —

He didn't finish the thought that what he needed to prove was that he had never, not for one afternoon, been one of them.

He didn't have to finish it. It drove him all the way to the bus without once saying its name.
