# The Echo — Craft Guardrails
_Read this before drafting or revising any scene or character beat. These are the durable
rules; the live canon (who/what/where) lives in the repo's `canon/` files._

## Voice & POV
- **Close third, anchored on Lucas (13).** Rationed leans to others only when earned.
- **Narrator at 13.** Lucas *feels*; he does not diagnose. No clinical self-insight about his
  own psychology. The reader understands dynamics *over his head*. (A review flagged an
  over-knowing narrator — keep the altitude young.)
- **At most one thesis line per scene.** Trust the image. Don't underline the moral the scene
  just delivered.

## The cardinal rule: don't preach
No character and no narrator delivers the lesson. If a line explains what a beat "means," cut it.

## Mess, not system
The magic system is legible and complete — that's the trap. In human/family beats keep the
machine off the page: no Catch / Read / Hold / Name / Choose verbs on display. Let scenes
occasionally *not* be about the design — a beat that's just love, a kid who solves out of lane,
a Nolan moment that exists only because he's a person.

## Lucas & representation
- **ADHD and dyslexia are lived** — cost plus non-magical texture — never labels or bullet points.
- **Lucas is not a secret seer.** He must sometimes be wrong about what's *true*, not only wrong
  about what to *do*, and a friend corrects him on the merits. If he's always perceptually right,
  the representation drifts to gifted-seer and the friends become unnecessary.

## Don't break Nolan (the emotional engine)
- Barbs are armor — show the cost to him underneath.
- Let him be right and sharp; his cruelty lands because it's partly true.
- Barbs punch up or sideways, never down at someone's vulnerability.
- As he grays he is **choosing**, not puppeted: the core dims while the surface (wit, charm) holds.
- Lucas's anger toward him stays **grief, never contempt**.
- Caution: lovable ≠ harmless.

## The midpoint logic (series spine)
Being *right* is what ruins Lucas: his certainty — shame curdled into righteousness — is the
blade. Don't have the author punish him; have his own rightness do the damage.

## The Accord / Devon
- The threat is **method, not malice**: the Gray is intended-as-mercy — the relief taken when
  you're named truly and you decline the costly choice.
- Devon should be magnetic enough that the reader half-agrees; withhold the dead-calm/"absence"
  tell until the midpoint pays it off.

## Resolutions
- **Win ≠ calm.** Resolutions preserve chaos and aliveness; the gray retreats from the mess.
- Option B (reckoning): naming reveals; a costly choice resolves, usually made by the afflicted
  person. Establish what their false self *buys* them so the choice costs something concrete.

## When in doubt
Prioritize the **Lucas–Nolan relationship** — it's the spine of the series and the thing every
review says determines whether the whole thing works.
