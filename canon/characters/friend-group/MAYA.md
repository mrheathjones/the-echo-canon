# Maya Sinclair

> Surname locked (proposed): **Sinclair**. Adjustable via single find-replace. Alternatives considered: Calloway, Ashford, Davenport.

Maya is one of Lucas's closest friends and a core member of the friend group.

Family background:
Maya comes from a long line of Ivy Leaguers and prominent professionals. Her father is a successful attorney. Her mother is an executive at a large, well-known company. Her family lives in a large, beautiful home in the rich neighborhood.

Maya's family name carries weight in town. Her paternal grandfather was a prominent doctor; her maternal grandfather was a successful businessman. This history drove her parents into their careers.

Schooling:
Her parents did not put her in private school because they want her exposed to the real world. They did pay for tutors and extracurricular learning, not because she is not smart, but because they wanted to offset what they saw as regular school's limitations. They stopped the extra learning about a year ago after seeing her burnout, but Maya still pushes herself.

Parental dynamic:
Her parents love her deeply and are trying to break generational cycles by not forcing prestige culture on her. They urge her to just be a kid and even ask if she wants to throw a party to engage socially. Her grandparents still pressure for private schools and status, causing tension.

Core Want: peace.
False Belief: if I make the wrong choice, I'll let people down.
Wound: fear of being the generation that fails to live up to the family story.
Strength: pattern recognition and perspective.
Weakness: trusting herself.
Flaw: analysis paralysis.

Personality:
Maya is thoughtful, observant, analytical, artistic, and deeply perceptive. She does not look down on others. In fact, she often feels uncomfortable with the pretentiousness of her social circle.

Relationship with Zoe:
Zoe is Maya's chosen sister. They are next-door neighbors, have been friends since preschool, and Zoe is one month older. Zoe protects Maya from the outside world; Maya gives Zoe a place where she does not have to be in charge.

Relationship with Lucas:
Lucas fascinates Maya. His chaotic family fascinates her. She envies the Hale family's mess and often tells Lucas he is lucky when he complains about siblings. Lucas's impulsiveness intrigues her because she cannot imagine blurting the first thing that comes to mind and surviving it.

Relationship with Mason:
Mason has been friends with Maya and Zoe for years. He feels like an older brother/cousin even though they are near the same age. Maya admires that he keeps moving forward and does not let roadblocks stop him, while reminding him this can be detrimental.

Relationship with Eli:
Maya and Eli share a quiet bond. They both get stuck in their heads. Maya sees Eli's loops and gently challenges them, then realizes she does the same thing.

Golf with Dad:
Maya is her dad's little golf buddy. She enjoys golf because it is slow, methodical, and lets her focus on one thing: hitting the ball. After golf they eat at the club and Maya orders the biggest sundae with the craziest toppings. This is their secret ritual.

At the Hale house:
Maya is family-adjacent. Nolan teases Lucas and Maya about being boyfriend/girlfriend. They deny it. Emma snaps at Nolan, Rachel tells him to leave them alone, Dylan roasts Nolan for not having a girlfriend, and Jack calls him immature.

## Engine role & distinct axis (distributed friend-group engine)

Engine role: **READER OF PEOPLE (READ).** Maya lands the true names — pattern and nuance no one else catches. Her flaw is the failure mode of reading: analysis paralysis and not trusting herself, so the true name often arrives late or stays unspoken until someone forces it out. See `canon/characters/friend-group/FRIEND_GROUP_ENGINE.md`.

Distinct axis (de-overlap vs Zoe and Eli): Maya's struggle is **judgment and the weight of choosing**, not control. Zoe wants to control *outcomes*; Eli wants to understand *mechanisms*; Maya wants to be *sure she won't make the wrong call and fail people.* Her paralysis is fear of a wrong choice, not a need to run the room. Keep her distinct on the page by playing the dread-of-deciding, not a control reflex.

Accord door: certainty / relief from the risk of being wrong.
